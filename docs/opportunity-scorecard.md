# Opportunity Scorecard

Scoring 1–10. **Higher is always better for us**, so two scales are inverted:
- **Competition** — 10 = whitespace, 1 = entrenched incumbent.
- **Reg. risk** — 10 = little regulatory downside for us, 1 = we would carry
  significant regulatory liability.

Weighted total uses: Pain ×1.5, WTP ×1.5, Competition ×1.5, AI leverage ×1.0,
Data ×1.0, Feasibility ×1.0, Acquisition ×1.25, Market ×1.0, Defensibility ×1.25,
Reg. risk ×0.75. Max 116.

Weights reflect the brief: pain, willingness to pay and competitive whitespace
dominate; "technically easy" is deliberately *not* weighted heavily.

## Score justification for the top 3

**#1 Waste permit conformance.** Competition 9 — 22 targeted searches found no
product validating movements against permitted waste codes; Defra's own provider
page confirms approval covers submission only. Data 9 — the register is a
verified open download and Standard Rules documents are published. Acquisition 8
— the founder has direct access to the industry through NRS. Defensibility 8 —
the extracted permit dataset is a genuine accumulating asset. Market 7 rather
than 9: 5,304 operators is enough for £1–10m ARR, not for £100m.

**#2 Phase 1 desk study.** AI leverage 9 — the deliverable is a written report
synthesised from documents, which is the single best-fitting task type available.
Data 6 — the historic map and environmental data comes from Landmark/Groundsure
at a per-report cost, so there is an input dependency on a data incumbent.
Competition 7 rather than 9 — no direct product found, but the ecology-report
equivalent (EcoScribe) proves the pattern is being noticed.

**#3 WM3 classification.** Competition 9 — searches surfaced only consultancies
(SOCOTEC, ECL, Alkali, The Testing Lab) and EWC *lookup* tools (WasteSupport,
Quick Consign, Wastebolt), never assessment software. Market 5 and Reg. risk 5
are what hold it back: WM3 assessment is a regulated technical judgement, and a
wrong hazardous/non-hazardous call has legal consequences we would be adjacent
to. It is a strong *module*, a weak *company*.
