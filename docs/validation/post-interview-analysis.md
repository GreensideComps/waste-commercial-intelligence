# Post-Interview Analysis

Complete **after all five interviews and after the 14-day follow-up window** —
not before. Working the numbers early invites you to stop when they look good.

## 1. Headline metrics

Non-control leads: **9**. Non-control observations: **20**.
Compute at both levels — they answer different questions.

| Metric | Formula |
|---|---|

## 2. Validity checks — run these before reading anything else

| Check | Threshold | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| Control (Card 7) scored C or D | **5 of 5** | 1 failure → discard that interview, recruit a replacement. **2+ failures → ABORT, result void** |
| ≥3 participants involved in winning work | | Result is indicative only; do not act on it |
| Card 10 enthusiasm | should be low | If a participant was keen on the weakest card, discount their interview |
| Interviewer "did I lead them?" | | Weight down any interview where you defended a lead |

⚠️ **If the validity checks fail, stop. Do not compute the headline metrics** —
you will believe them anyway.

## 3. Which signal are we actually in?

Group the leads that came back unknown **and** actionable, and see where they
cluster. The clustering matters more than the count, because it tells you what
to narrow to.

| # | Signal type | Leads that would indicate it | What it implies |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Broad waste intelligence** | Spread evenly across all types | Widest market, weakest differentiation, closest to Barbour |
| 2 | **Industrial / remediation intelligence** | 2 Sinfin A, 3 Sinfin D, 6 Alpha Anodizing, 9 Hermitage Mill | Narrow, defensible, matches NRS's stated specialism. **The most likely good outcome** |
| 3 | **Infrastructure intelligence** | 1 HS2/EKFB | High value, few buyers, procurement-led — closer to a bid-monitoring service |
| 4 | **Hazardous / specialist waste intelligence** | 6 Alpha Anodizing, 2 Sinfin A | Highest £/tonne, smallest volumes, most specialist |
| 5 | **Something the interviews revealed** | — | ⚠️ **Watch for this.** If three of five independently describe a different problem, that is worth more than the whole planned test |

**Write the answer as one sentence** before doing anything else:

> "The signal is ______________, evidenced by ______________."

If you cannot complete that sentence, there is no signal.

---

## 4. Interpretation traps

- **Do not average away a bimodal result.** "50% unknown" from two enthusiasts
  and three flat rejections is not a moderate positive; it is disagreement, and
  you should find out which group is right.
- **Do not count C as a weak A.** It is a different product signal, and it routes
  to CHANGE THE PRODUCT.
- **Do not read a high "can't tell" rate on value as neutral.** It means the
  product cannot yet do the job it promises.
- **Do not let one enthusiastic participant carry the result.** Check whether the
  positives come from one person; if so, the sample is effectively n=1.
- **Do not discount the 14-day silence.** It is data, and it is the most honest
  data in the study.

## 5. Write-up

One page, in this order — conclusion first, so you cannot bury it:

1. **The decision** (from `decision-rule.md`, no re-negotiation)
2. NNAL and the validity checks
3. The signal-type sentence
4. The three most surprising verbatim quotes
5. What you now know about the incumbent (cost, sources, lead time, haul radius)
6. What you would do differently in the next test
7. **Everything you were wrong about** — list it explicitly

Then append the result to `progress.md` and stop. Do not begin building in the
same session as the analysis; sleep on it.
