# NRS — Opportunity Discovery Test
### Evidence pack · Prepared 9 September 2026 · All records re-verified same day

---

## Cover note (read this out, or hand it over first)

I ran a test to see whether **public information alone** — planning
applications, procurement notices — can find waste-generating opportunities in
our patch.

**What I did:** scanned roughly 4,000 planning records across every local
authority within 110 km of Meriden, for applications lodged between May and
September 2026, filtered to those with contamination, remediation, demolition
or ground investigation conditions. **442 fell inside our catchment.** Five of
them are in this pack.

**What I want from you:** for each one, tell me whether we already knew about
it, and whether we'd actually do anything about it. **"We already knew that" is
the most useful answer you can give me** — it tells me this isn't worth
pursuing, which is what I'm trying to establish.

**Two things I have deliberately not done:**

- **No tonnages and no revenue figures.** They cannot be derived from planning
  records — the volumes sit inside submitted reports I could not access. Any
  number I put here would be invented, so there are none. The single £ figure in
  this pack (Card 5) is quoted directly from the published notice.
- **No ranking or scoring.** I don't want to tell you which ones I think are
  good before you tell me what you think.

**How to read the cards:** **FACT** is quoted from a named public source.
**INTERPRETATION** is my reasoning, and I may be wrong.

**Every card has live links.** Open them in the meeting — that's better evidence
than anything I could print.

### WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DISCOVERED

Rolls-Royce holds a planning permission (**26/00060/FUL**) **specifically for

⚠️ **Worth knowing how hard this was to find:** a normal internet search for
application 26/00060/FUL returned nothing — the search engine reported it "may
be a very recent 2026 application that might not yet be widely indexed online."
It took a direct query of the planning database to surface it.

- Decades of aero-engine manufacturing on the site would typically mean
  hydrocarbons, chlorinated solvents, heavy metals and cutting oils in the ground.
- A remediation strategy submitted for approval normally precedes physical works
  by weeks to a few months.
- AECOM, as author of the strategy, would ordinarily influence where material goes.
- **I do not know the volume.** It is not stated in the planning record.

### WHY THIS COULD MATTER TO NRS

Hydrocarbon and heavy-metal impacted soil of the type we treat rather than
landfill — and the strategy is still being written, so the disposal route may
not be fixed.

# CARD 2
## Alpha Anodizing & Polishing Ltd — Bond End, Yoxall, DE13 8NL

### FACTS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Planning reference** | **P/2026/00884** — East Staffordshire Borough Council |
| **Date received** | **14 August 2026** |
| **Status** | **Undecided — decision recorded as PENDING** as at 9 September 2026 |
| **Appeal reference** | **APP/B3410/W/23/3332984** (permission granted on appeal) |
| **Company** | Alpha Anodizing & Polishing Ltd — Companies House **06979528** |
| **Distance from Meriden** | approx. 38 km |

**Verbatim from the planning record:**

> *"Discharge of Condition number 9d (**remediation works**) and 9e
> (**verification report**) of planning permission allowed on appeal
> APP/B3410/W/23/3332984 relating to **demolition of commercial works to
> facilitate the erection of 8 No. dwellinghouses** including landscaping"*

### WHAT WAS ACTUALLY DISCOVERED

A working metal-finishing plant is being demolished for housing, and the
remediation works condition is with the council right now.

⚠️ **Targeted searching for this site returned only a Facebook page and business
directory listings.** The appeal and the remediation condition did not appear.
This is the sort of thing that is effectively invisible unless someone is
watching planning data systematically.

### INTERPRETATION (my reasoning — not fact)

- Anodising and metal polishing use sulphuric and chromic acid baths and caustic
  etch, and generate heavy-metal bearing residues.
- Soils on such sites commonly contain **chromium, nickel and aluminium**, with
  acidic residues, and depending on process history possibly cyanide-bearing
  material.
- Demolition of an industrial building of this age may involve asbestos.
- **Only 8 houses, so the site is small — volume is likely modest.** I have not
  estimated it. The interest here is the *hazard class*, not the quantity.

### WHY THIS COULD MATTER TO NRS

Heavy-metal contaminated soil is exactly the specialist stream we're set up for,
and it is the sort of material that needs treatment rather than a landfill
ticket. Small volume, potentially high value per tonne.

### SOURCES — open these live

- Planning record: **https://www.planit.org.uk/planapplic/EastStaffordshire/P/2026/00884/**
- East Staffordshire portal: https://eaststaffs-publicportal.statmap.co.uk/horizoNext/publicportal/planningapplications/644192
- Companies House: https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/06979528

**Confidence: HIGH that remediation is required. MEDIUM-HIGH on contaminant
type. LOW on volume.**

These five came from a pool of **442 applications in our catchment**, from a
four-month window. Five more of comparable quality were set aside to keep this
conversation to half an hour — including a **Large** application at Woodville,
Swadlincote (ref DMOT/2026/0939) on former opencast land where the **consultation
closes 23 September 2026**.

**What I honestly could not do:**
- Work out tonnages or values — those sit in submitted reports behind council
  document portals, and one council's portal wouldn't respond at all.
- Identify the contractor on most of them. **Around 6 in 10 planning records
  don't name anyone** we could ring.

I'd rather you knew both of those now.
