# Interview Script — 25 to 30 minutes

**Materials:** one pack of five cards (folded), the capture sheet from
`data-collection.md`, a pen. **Not** a laptop, and **not** a slide deck.

**Timing:** Part 1 ≈ 6 min · Part 2 ≈ 4 min × 5 cards = 20 min · Part 3 ≈ 6 min.

---

## Opening — read this, near enough verbatim

> "Thanks for the time. I've been testing whether public information —
> planning applications, tender notices, environmental records — can find waste
> opportunities that a good commercial team wouldn't already have.
>
> I've got ten. You're going to see five of them. For each one I'll ask whether
> you already knew about it.
>
> **The most useful answer for me is 'yes, we knew that.'** That tells me the
> idea doesn't work, which is genuinely what I'm trying to find out. I'm not
> selling you anything and there's nothing to buy. If you're polite about it,
> you'll cost me money.
>
> Two labels on the cards: **FACT** means it comes from a named public source.
> **INFERENCE** means it's my reasoning, and I could be wrong. There are no
> tonnages or values on the cards, because I couldn't get them from public data
> and I'm not going to make them up."

**Then say nothing else about the idea until Part 3.**

---

## PART 1 — How things work today (6 min, before any card)

Ask these first. Once a participant has seen a card, their description of their
own process is coloured by it.

**Q1.1** "How do new commercial opportunities normally reach you? Walk me
through the last three you can remember."

**Q1.2** "Which sources or subscriptions do you use? Barbour ABI, Glenigan,
anything else?" *(If yes: what does it do well, where does it let you down?)*

**Q1.3** "How far ahead do you normally hear about work? Weeks, months, a year?"

**Q1.4** "Who actually does this research? Is it someone's job, or does it fit
around other work?"

**Q1.5** "Roughly how much time goes into it in a week — across everyone?"

**Q1.6** "What makes an opportunity worth chasing rather than noting? Is there a
size or distance below which you wouldn't bother?"

**Q1.7** "How far will you economically haul contaminated soil? Does that change
by waste type?"
*→ Needed regardless of outcome; a blocking unknown in `docs/risks.md` §3.*

---

## PART 2 — The cards (4 min each, one at a time)

Hand over **Side 1** only. Let them read it fully. Then, in this order:

### 🔒 ASK BEFORE ANY DISCUSSION

**Q1 — "Before today, did you know about this?"**

Record one letter — do not paraphrase:

| | |
|---|---|
| **A** | Never heard of it |
| **B** | Had vaguely heard of the project |
| **C** | Knew the project, but not this waste opportunity |
| **D** | Already actively pursuing it |

*If they hesitate between B and C, ask: "Did you know there was contaminated
material coming off it?" That distinction is the single most important
measurement in this test.*

**Q2 — "If you knew: how, and roughly when?"**
*Capture the channel verbatim — Barbour, trade press, a customer, a contractor,
word of mouth, the site is local.*

### → Now unfold Side 2 and let them read it.

**Q3** "Now you've seen the rest — is this useful information?"

**Q4** "Would NRS actually pursue this? Yes, no, or not yet — and why?"

**Q5** "Who specifically would you contact?"
*A name, a company, or a role. "The developer" is not sufficient.*

**Q6** "Roughly what would this be worth to NRS if it converted?
Under £10k / £10–50k / £50–250k / £250k+ / can't tell from this."
*If "can't tell" — ask what's missing. **That answer defines the product.***

**Q7** "Would you have expected to find this through your existing sources?
If so, when — and would it have been in time?"

**Q8** "What would stop you acting on it?"

**Q9** "Would you pay for a service that consistently surfaced things like this?"
*Do not name a price. Do not accept "maybe" — ask "what would it have to do?"*

⏱ **If a card runs past 5 minutes, move on.** Five thin readings beat three
thorough ones plus two rushed.

---

## PART 3 — Closing (6 min)

**Q3.1** "Of the five, how many were genuinely new to you?"
*Then check it against your own tally. Discrepancies are informative.*

**Q3.2** "If leads like these arrived reliably — how many a week could the team
actually work? What's the point where it becomes noise?"

**Q3.3** "What would you need to see before you'd act on one of these without
checking it yourself first?"

### The willingness-to-pay sequence — ask in this order, do not skip ahead

**Q3.4 — current spend** "What do you currently pay for market or project
intelligence, all in — subscriptions, memberships, anything?"

**Q3.5 — current labour** "If someone spends X hours a week on this, what does
that cost NRS in a year, roughly?"

**Q3.6 — consultant spend** "Do you ever pay consultants for market or site
research? What does that typically cost?"

**Q3.7 — outcome anchor** "What would this have to deliver in a year for it to
be obviously worth paying for? One extra job? Ten?"

**Q3.8 — the price question (they go first)** "If this delivered five to ten
leads a week at roughly the quality of the best two you've seen today — what
would you expect to pay per month?"
*If they ask what it costs: **"That's exactly what I'm trying to work out — what
would you expect?"** Do not name a figure. Ever.*

**Q3.9 — model** "Would a subscription suit you, or would you rather pay per
lead, or a commission on work won?"

**Q3.10 — budget reality** "Who would sign that off? And what would you stop
spending on to pay for it?"
*The second half is the real test — a budget with nothing displaced is a
hypothetical budget.*

### The disconfirming close — do not omit

**Q3.11** "What would make this useless to you?"

**Q3.12** "If I built this and came back in six months, what's the most likely
reason you wouldn't be using it?"

### 🎯 The revealed-preference ask — the most important question in the script

**Q3.13** "Would you like me to send you these five as a document you could
actually act on?"

*Say nothing more. Do not offer to help. Note the answer.*

**Then do nothing for 14 days.** If they contact a counterparty on one of these
leads without further prompting from you, that single fact outweighs everything
else recorded today (`decision-rule.md` §Tie-breaker). Log it on the follow-up
row of the capture sheet.

---

## Immediately after — before you speak to anyone

Spend five minutes alone and write:

1. **Your gut score: how much of that was real?** (1–10)
2. **The most surprising thing they said.**
3. **Anything you said that you shouldn't have** — did you defend a lead, fill a
   silence, or lead them? Note it; it affects how you weight the interview.
4. **Did the control land correctly?** If not, flag the interview for discard
   now, not later when the numbers are disappointing.
