# Interview Sheet — one page, keep this to yourself

**Purpose: find out whether NRS already knew these, and whether we'd act.
Not to get approval for anything.**

## Before you start — say this

> "I'm testing whether public data can find opportunities we'd otherwise miss.
> There are five. For each one I want to know if we already knew about it.
> **'We knew that' is the most useful answer you can give me** — it tells me to
> stop. Please don't be kind about it."

Then: **no opinions from you until all five are done.**

## Three rules

1. **Ask "did you already know?" before turning to the interpretation section.**
2. **Never say which one you think is best.**
3. **Don't defend a card if it gets dismissed.** Write down why and move on.

---

## Per card — ask in this order

**① Did you already know about this?** *(circle one — ask before discussing)*

| A | Never heard of it |
|---|---|
| **B** | Vaguely aware of the project |
| **C** | **Knew the project — but not that this waste work was happening** |
| **D** | Already chasing it |

→ *If they hesitate between B and C, ask:* **"Did you know there was contaminated
material coming off it?"** — **this distinction is the single most important
thing on the sheet.**

**② If you knew — how, and roughly when?**

**③ Would NRS actually do anything about this?** Yes / No / Not yet — and why?

**④ Who exactly would you contact?** *(a name, company or role — "the developer"
isn't enough)*

**⑤ Roughly what's it worth if it lands?** <£10k / £10–50k / £50–250k / £250k+ /
can't tell → *if "can't tell", ask what's missing*

**⑥ Would you have found this through what we already use? When?**

**⑦ What would stop us acting on it?**

## At the end

**⑧** "Of those five, how many were genuinely new to you?"

**⑨** "How do opportunities normally reach you now?" *(customers / contractors /
tenders / Barbour or similar / word of mouth)*

**⑩** "Do we pay for anything like Barbour ABI or Glenigan? What does it do well,
where does it let us down?"

**⑪** "Who does this kind of research here, and how much time goes into it?"

**⑫** "How far will we economically haul contaminated soil? Does Derby work?"
*→ Needed whatever the outcome.*

**⑬** "If five to ten of these arrived every week, how many could the team
actually work before it became noise?"

**⑭** "What would make this useless to us?" *(let the silence run)*

**⑮ The one that counts:** **"Would you like these five as something you can act
on?"**
→ Then **say nothing further and do nothing for two weeks.** If anyone actually
rings one of these counterparties without you prompting again, that matters more
than everything else on this page.

## 🔒 Interpreting it afterwards — for you, not for the room

**Card 3 (Mell Square) is a control.** 1,600 homes, Muse and GRAHAM, 9 km away,
covered in the trade press. **They should score it B, C or D.** If your manager
has never heard of it, they're not close enough to the commercial pipeline for
the rest of their answers to mean much — note that and treat the session as
indicative only.

**What counts as genuinely new:** **A** and **C** only.
**B is not new** — if they can't say how and when they heard, downgrade it to A.
**D is not new.**

**C is the interesting result.** If they knew the projects but not that the waste
work was live, then the value isn't finding projects — it's spotting the waste
event inside projects everyone can already see. That's a different product, and
it would mean Barbour is a supplier rather than a competitor.

**"That's interesting" counts as nothing.** A card only counts as actionable if
they give you **why, who, what and when** without being led. Write INTERESTING in
the box if that's all you got, and count them up later.

**Rough read:**

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| 3+ scored A or C **and** they name who to call | Worth taking further |
| Mostly C | The value is interpretation, not discovery — different product |
| 1–2 new, all one type (e.g. industrial remediation) | Narrow to that niche |
| Mostly B or D | We already know this stuff. Stop. |
| Warm words, nobody rings anyone in two weeks | Treat as a no |

**Write your answers down in the room.** Not afterwards from memory — that's when
you'll remember it more favourably than it was.
